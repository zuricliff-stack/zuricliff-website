Before You Travel

A little paperwork, done early, so it never crosses your mind again.

  • Passport: valid for at least six months after your arrival date.
  • Visa: apply online in advance for the e-Visa at visa.immigration.go.tz (USD 50 for most nationalities, USD 100 for US citizens). Please use only the official .go.tz website.
  • Mandatory travel insurance: all foreign visitors except residents must have inbound travel insurance (USD 44 per visitor, valid for 92 days). One policy covers the whole journey; you buy it for your first port of entry. If Zanzibar is your first port of entry: inbound.visitzanzibar.go.tz. If you arrive on the mainland first: inbound.nicinsurance.co.tz. Without it you may be refused entry. It is a local requirement and does not replace your own travel and health insurance; we recommend having both.
  • Return ticket: please have your return or onward ticket ready to show at border control.
  • Yellow fever: a vaccination certificate is required only if you arrive from, or spend 12 hours or more in transit in, a country with a risk of yellow fever.
  • Health: please ask your doctor or a travel clinic in good time about malaria prevention and any other precautions for the region.

House Rules

The villa is a private home, and we ask you to treat it as you would your own. Adults only, 18 and over. Smoking is permitted outdoors only. Quiet hours are from 22:00 to 8:00. No pets, parties or events. Please take care around the infinity pool and the cliff edge, particularly when the deck is wet.

Getting Around

Many guests rent a car. It is the easiest way to explore the island at your own pace, and your host is happy to recommend a rental company. For something more local, there are taxis, tuk-tuks and boda-bodas (motorbike taxis, also handy for groceries or food delivery), and, for excursions further afield, a driver. Just ask your host for a contact; you arrange and pay the driver directly, not through us.

There is parking at the side of the villa, by the entrance, with room for several cars. You are welcome to leave a car there for the length of your stay.

Things to Do

  • Dolphins, Kizimkazi: boat trips to see wild dolphins, about 20 minutes from the villa.
  • Jozani Forest: Zanzibar's only national park, home to the rare red colobus monkey.
  • Butterfly Centre: see colourful butterflies up close.
  • Mangroves: paddle a canoe through the mangroves.
  • Spice tour: a guided walk through a working spice farm, tasting cloves, vanilla, cinnamon and more.
  • Stone Town: Zanzibar's old town and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, about 90 minutes away, with its history, markets and sunsets on the waterfront.
  • Kitesurfing, Paje: a popular kitesurfing destination, with lessons for all levels.
  • Pungume Island: a day out by boat to the island.
  • Mtende Beach and Usamba Rock: a hidden beach and cliff in the south.
  • Sunset on the west side: Fahari Off The Grid, Kizimkazi; Kizikula Hotel, Kizimkazi (minimum spend USD 40 per person); or Michamvi Bay.

Restaurants and Bars Nearby

From local food and relaxed beachfront restaurants to lively evenings, here is a selection for your stay.

Near Makunduchi

  • Eden Rock, Mtende: a small eco-lodge restaurant on a rocky headland above Mtende Beach, with grilled fish and local dishes.
  • The One Resort Zanzibar: the resort restaurant in Kigaeni, Makunduchi, with Swahili, seafood and barbecue evenings.

Jambiani and the south-east coast

  • BeZanzibar: a relaxed place for food and drinks along the coast.
  • Bamboo: the restaurant of Bamboo Zanzibar, a small beach hotel in Jambiani.
  • Joud: the restaurant of Joud by the Sea, a beachfront hotel in Jambiani.
  • Passion Boutique Hotel: Zanzibari flavours meet international cooking, with seafood and handmade pasta, in the garden or on the rooftop, in Jambiani.
  • The Loop: the restaurant of The Loop Beach Resort, on the beach in Jambiani.
  • Mwezi: international cooking in a laid-back coastal atmosphere.
  • Bomwa: a local bistro in Jambiani with creative, locally inspired small plates.
  • Gerezani: Zanzibari cooking with a modern twist, such as coconut tuna curry, in Jambiani.
  • Marea: Italian cooking at Marea Boutique Hotel, Jambiani.
  • Rafiki's Chickies: chicken in every form, from burgers to wings.

Paje: beachfront dining

About 20 minutes from Zuri Cliff, Paje has a lively beach atmosphere with plenty of restaurants, cafés and beach bars.

  • Mr Kahawa: a beachfront café with specialty coffee, fresh juices, salads and plant-based dishes. Good for breakfast or a relaxed afternoon by the beach.
  • ZanziBarista: a café and bar on Paje Beach for Tanzanian coffee, Swahili snacks and drinks, open daily.
  • Shanga: a restaurant and bar in Paje, relaxed during the day and livelier on Saturday evenings with a house DJ and djembe drums.
  • Beach Bohème: the restaurant of a small beach hotel, with African, Mediterranean and grilled dishes.
  • Al Casbah: North African and Algerian cooking, such as tajine and couscous.
  • Mosaiik: Turkish and Moroccan-inspired cooking.
  • Basil: modern Asian cooking.
  • Ocean Restaurant: seafood platters and the fish of the day.

Coffee and breakfast

  • BaraBara, Paje: a café for coffee and a relaxed breakfast, with pizza in the evening.
  • ASILI Goods Cafe, Paje: a café and bar between the Kijiji supermarket and the Bento food court.

Further along the coast

  • Baladin, Pingwe: a beach hotel restaurant with a Peruvian chef, and Baladin craft beer at the swim-up bar.
  • Upendo Beach, Michamvi: a beach hotel known for its Boho Social Sunday Brunch.

Practical Information

  • Currency: the Tanzanian shilling (TZS); US dollars are widely accepted. Small businesses and local vendors often prefer cash.
  • ATMs: in Jambiani and Paje.
  • Shopping: Jambiani Plaza has a supermarket for drinks and snacks; The Bazaar on Main Road, Jambiani, has a shop for fresh fruit and vegetables.
  • Time zone: East Africa Time (EAT), UTC+3.
  • Power sockets: Type D and G, 230 V.
  • Tides: the tide here goes out a long way, and beach and swimming conditions change a great deal between low and high tide. Ask your host for the best swimming times during your stay.
  • Tipping: not expected, but appreciated for good service. As a guide: TZS 5,000 to 10,000 (about USD 2 to 4) per trip for drivers, and at the end of your stay whatever you feel is right for the team.

Local Etiquette

Zanzibar is predominantly Muslim, relaxed and respectful. Swimwear is perfectly fine at the villa, by the pool and on the beach; in the villages, a light cover-up is appreciated. People are warm and welcoming, and a friendly ‘Jambo’ goes a long way. ‘Asante’ means thank you.
